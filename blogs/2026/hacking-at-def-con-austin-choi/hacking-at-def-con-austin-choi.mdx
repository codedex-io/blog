**Hacking.**

A word many people fear, or at least keeps them at a distance. But what does it really mean?

At its core, it's simply looking at something and seeing how it works, what makes it tick, and seeing what you can do with that.

That was me this last summer, surrounded by 30,000+ other people doing the exact same thing, at [DEF CON](https://defcon.org), the world's largest and most iconic hacking conference, held annually in Las Vegas.

### Hacking Conference???

Yep, it's exactly as it seems. A conference where the world's preeminent hackers gather together to hack things, and more importantly... party and have a blast! 🥳

So for those of you who don't know, DEF CON was started by [Jeff "The Dark Tangent" Moss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Moss_(hacker)) back in 1993 as a way to say farewell to a hacker friend of his. He brought 100 hackers with him to Las Vegas so they could party together and somehow, this ended up becoming a tradition that eventually grew into what it is today, a conference that boasts tens of thousands of attendees from all over the world, representing various companies like Google, Microsoft, etc., agencies like the FBI, NSA, DOE, CISA, etc., and hacker groups that will go unnamed.

Where did the name DEF CON even come from, some of you may wonder. 

Some of you in the US may know about **DEFCON** in the military, which stands for the Defense Readiness Condition, a scale to indicate the level of military threat against the US. Jeff Moss took this after it got popularized in the 1983 movie, WarGames. He also cites that he used the old phone keypad where the number 3 would represent the letters DEF as an homage to old school phone phreakers, people who hacked phone lines. Con is just short for conference, nothing really deep here lol.

DEF CON is usually held in the first week or second week of August, which is also known as the Hacker Summer Camp, a week with 3 of the world's biggest cybersecurity conferences all in Las Vegas: BSidesLV, Black Hat, and DEF CON. Nowadays, DEF CON is held in the Las Vegas Convention Center, but years before it would be held across multiple casinos like the Caesars Forum, the LINQ, Harrah's, etc.

### The Villages

DEF CON is split into what are called Villages. These are groups of people that share interests in subcategories of cybersecurity. 

For example:

- **Red Team Village:** A village all about offensive security and hacking.
- **Crypto and Privacy Village:** A group of math obsessed people trying to obfuscate whatever they can.
- **Aerospace Village:** A space full of planes and rockets that you can actually hack.
- **Car Hacking Village:** The same as Aerospace but with cars lol.
- **Lockpicking Village:** Where you can learn how to be like the Lock Picking Lawyer (just maybe not as good).
- **Badge Life Village:** Where people share the badges they made and collected over the years (more on badges later).

### How did I get here?

Thanks to my involvement with the UNLV cybersecurity orgs, I managed to go to DEF CON for the first time in 2023 where I got my first taste of real hacker culture. I got to experience CTFs (Capture the Flag competitions, more on those later), Workshops, the Wall of Sheep which shamed people who logged into things over the conference wifi without encryption by putting their usernames and blurred passwords on a giant screen, and so much more. 

It was an absolute blast and I couldn't get enough. So, ever since then, I ended up going several more years.

### Badges

So you might be wondering, what's so important about badges that warrants an entire village?

Well badges aren't simply a way to get into the conference, they're a way of life. Back in DEF CON 14 (2006), Jeff Moss decided to spice things up a bit and introduced the first DEF CON electronic badge.

<img src="https://firebasestorage.googleapis.com/v0/b/codedex-io.appspot.com/o/community%2Finline%2FazFKkpxOJne2uJXurpc8Is8K4eI3%2FxtwVMdF6o3O0aYx1d1Ha%2F1791873694052_defcon-14-badge.webp?alt=media&token=1577af29-ec94-472e-94a1-114a7b99dbfb" alt="" width="100%"></img>

Ever since then, every other year, DEF CON issues out electronic badges that come with some cool features that attendees can hack with, hack into, and just mess around with. I actually have 2 electronic badges myself, and this year's badge is one of them.

Thanks to this, people started creating their own custom electronic badges with cool features. Some of these include badges like a wearable Pip-Boy badge, a laser tag badge, a Flock camera blocking badge, and so much more.

I managed to wrangle myself a decent collection just from this year.

<img src="https://firebasestorage.googleapis.com/v0/b/codedex-io.appspot.com/o/community%2Finline%2FazFKkpxOJne2uJXurpc8Is8K4eI3%2FxtwVMdF6o3O0aYx1d1Ha%2F1791874006164_defcon-34-badges.webp?alt=media&token=d3761f8c-bac8-410d-a043-2845bfc3ea4f" alt="" width="100%"></img>

I even got a badge that acts like a D20. Guess I know what I'll be using next D&D night.

My favorite badge from this year though, was that interesting looking badge with a numpad on it. This was the 5N4CK3Y (SNACKEY) badge that people can only get by completing a CTF that makes you "hack" into a vending machine.

### Capture the Flag!!!

One of the staples in the cybersecurity community is CTFs. These are competitions where people solve cybersecurity challenges and submit flags in order to get points or reach some objective. DEF CON is full of them and they can range anywhere from the most beginner friendly ones to extremely complex ones that would even make Claude Fable and GPT-6 Astra curl up into the fetal position on their therapist's couch.

5N4CK3Y was one that some friends and I did in order to win the 5N4CK3Y badge. It consisted of radio frequency challenges, cryptography, and many more topics.

<img src="https://firebasestorage.googleapis.com/v0/b/codedex-io.appspot.com/o/community%2Finline%2FazFKkpxOJne2uJXurpc8Is8K4eI3%2FxtwVMdF6o3O0aYx1d1Ha%2F1791874945381_snackey.webp?alt=media&token=80075410-deb6-4bbf-a54c-1543c83ce7c6" alt="" width="100%"></img>

Another CTF I did, and absolutely loved, was Gold Bug by Crypto and Privacy Village, where attendees roleplay as a detective to solve a mystery. These are easily some of the most difficult cryptography challenges I've ever come across, granted that isn't saying much. It consisted of challenges where you have to analyze an irl art gallery the village set up in order to solve puzzles online. I barely made any progress at all so eh, here's to next year.

<img src="https://firebasestorage.googleapis.com/v0/b/codedex-io.appspot.com/o/community%2Finline%2FazFKkpxOJne2uJXurpc8Is8K4eI3%2FxtwVMdF6o3O0aYx1d1Ha%2F1791933936044_cpv-art-gallery.webp?alt=media&token=dbad2e0a-b106-45b7-b1e6-477dc491e401" alt="" width="100%"></img>

As much as I wanted to compete in more challenges, I couldn't get to all of the ones I wanted to. But there was one I didn't do that I would argue is one of my favorite CTFs at the entire conference, the "? Cube", created by Las Vegas' very own DEF CON group DC702.

### Nothing like hanging out with friends
Over the last year, I got close with the local Las Vegas DEF CON group, going to their weekly meetings and just having fun. Thanks to this I just got to hang out and have fun after the conference hours ended, just talking, drinking some soju, and just relaxing. One thing many new DEF CON attendees tend to forget is that DEF CON started as a party, and is still at its core a party, just a really big one now.

I'd be hanging out with the DC702 guys, friends from school, and random strangers from all walks of life. We would go to the dance floor and enjoy the music, go to a hotel room to play some pool, and just enjoy ourselves.

### Conclusion

With that said, DEF CON is such a fun experience. It's chaotic and exciting. There's something for everyone, whether you're a beginner script kiddie or a seasoned veteran of the cyber arts. 

If you ever have the chance to attend DEF CON whether it's from work, volunteering, or presenting, take that chance and GO.

Maybe I'll see you there next year. 🙂
