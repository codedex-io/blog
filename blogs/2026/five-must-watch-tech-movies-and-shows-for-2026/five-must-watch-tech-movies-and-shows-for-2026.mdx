## 1. The Social Reckoning (Film)

<iframe width="560" height="315" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/gM4LkaXwGuY?si=cDbZH7GfVqP_Y59I" title="YouTube video player" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" allowfullscreen></iframe>

Aaron Sorkin returns to the world of _The Social Network_, this time to examine the consequences of Facebook's success after a decade. It focuses on events that gave rise to _Wall Street Journal_’s shocking exposé, [The Facebook Files](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Facebook_leak). It's inspired by the true story of how Frances Haugen, a young Meta engineer, enlists the help of Jeff Horwitz, a WSJ reporter, to go on a dangerous journey that ends up blowing the whistle on the social network’s most guarded secrets.

Jeremy Strong (_Succession_) portrays Zuck, with Mikey Madison (_Anora_) as Haugen and Jeremy Allen White (_The Bear_, _Shameless_) as Horwitz. Wait, is that... Bill Burr (_Breaking Bad_)?

**Why watch**: It's no longer about building Facebook. It's about what happens when the platform becomes too powerful to ignore. But too bad Jesse Eisenberg isn't in it.

- **Release:** October 9, 2026
- **Runtime:** 1h 52m
- **Director:** Aaron Sorkin
- **Studio:** Sony Pictures

## 2. You Can See Everything (Documentary)

<iframe width="560" height="315" src="https://www.youtube-nocookie.com/embed/HvW_N5p1q44?si=yG0hYpm-En5xvGB3" title="YouTube video player" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" allowfullscreen></iframe>

What begins as a documentary following [Elizabeth Holmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Holmes), founder of Theranos, 34 days before she's sent to federal prison becomes a mind-bending three-year journey into the abyss. Featured on Rotten Tomatoes' The Most Anticipated Movies of 2026.

**Why watch**: Everyone has gone down the Theranos scandal rabbit hole at least once in their life. And they let Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim loose on this story?! I'm a HUGE _Nathan for You_ fan and I just saw Lance Oppenheim's _Primetime_, which was incredible. BEYOND excited for this one. Every screening at my go-to theater, [Nitehawk](https://nitehawkcinema.com/prospectpark/), has been sold out. Ugh.

- **Release:** October 16, 2026
- **Runtime:** 2h 54m
- **Directors:** Nathan Fielder & Lance Oppenheim
- **Studio:** A24

## 3. Musk (Documentary)

<iframe width="560" height="315" src="https://www.youtube-nocookie.com/embed/FFTf1lzcs28?si=V8AzNDW1tuDHZ2SN" title="YouTube video player" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" allowfullscreen></iframe>

The world's most polarizing tech ~billionaire~ trillionaire gets the documentary treatment. Oscar-winning filmmaker Alex Gibney explores Elon Musk's rise, his companies, his public persona, and his expanding influence across technology and politics. Running approximately four hours, this is anything but a quick biography.

**Why watch**: Whether you're fascinated by Tesla and SpaceX or skeptical of Musk's influence, this documentary promises a critical look at the man behind the empire. I might skip this one tbh since I've read his books and tired of hearing about him.

- **Release:** October 16, 2026
- **Runtime:** 3h 45m
- **Director:** Alex Gibney
- **Studio:** Jigsaw Productions

## 4. The Altruists (Netflix Series)

<iframe width="560" height="315" src="https://www.youtube-nocookie.com/embed/9aGeKSSyuhk?si=iMY4SE2YRDcsP-uT" title="YouTube video player" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" allowfullscreen></iframe>

Netflix dives into the rise and fall of [FTX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FTX), the cryptocurrency exchange once valued in the billions. Anthony Boyle (_House of Guinness_) plays Sam Bankman-Fried aka SBF, while Julia Garner (_Ozark_, _Inventing Anna_) portrays Caroline Ellison. The eight-episode series explores their relationship, ambition, and the scandal that brought the crypto empire down.

**Why watch**: I don't know much about the SBF story, so definitely gonna watch this!

- **Release:** November 19, 2026
- **Runtime:** 8 episodes
- **Studio:** Higher Ground Productions

## 5. Artificial (Film)

<iframe width="560" height="315" src="https://www.youtube-nocookie.com/embed/rDZplZFnbOk?si=doOI9TCqTX9wGgyr" title="YouTube video player" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" allowfullscreen></iframe>

Remember when OpenAI CEO [Sam Altman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Altman) was suddenly fired in November 2023, only to return days later? Director Luca Guadagnino turns that extraordinary corporate crisis into a feature film, starring Andrew Garfield (_The Amazing Spider-Man_, _The Social Network_) as Altman. This movie was dropped by Amazon and indie distributor NEON saved it for its release.

**Why watch**: It's a story about power, trust, corporate governance, and who gets to control the future of artificial intelligence. I'm also reading _Empire of AI_ by Karen Hao, so I'm hyped.

- **Release:** December 25, 2026
- **Runtime:** 2h 20m
- **Director:** Luca Guadagnino

## Conclusion

It does make me a little sad that this is the world we live in now.

All five of these focus on the darker side of Big Tech – power struggles, scandals, greed, and broken trust. And while these stories will undoubtedly make for compelling entertainment, they'll also shape how mainstream audiences perceive the tech industry.

I miss the days when tech stories were about innovation, creativity, and the excitement of building something that could change the world for the better. Those stories still exist, of course, but they seem to be getting overshadowed by cautionary tales.

As much as I'm excited to watch most of these, I hope we continue to shine a light on the good that's still happening in our own corner of tech, especially in the startup space.
